# Keyword Research Playbook

## Phase 1: Seed ideas (no tools yet)

Start with the customer's language, not tool output.

- List every service the business offers, in plain words a customer would use.
- List every problem the service solves ("leaking tap", "broken boiler", "tooth pain").
- List every location served (city, neighborhoods, nearby towns).
- Combine: service x problem x location gives the first few hundred seeds.
- Mine: Google autocomplete, People Also Ask, Related Searches, competitor titles, customer reviews (the words customers actually use), sales call notes.

Write everything down. Do not filter yet.

## Phase 2: Expand and measure

For each seed, pull data from:

- **Google Keyword Planner** — volume ranges, free with a Google Ads account.
- **Keywords Everywhere** — volume and CPC in the SERP sidebar.
- **Google Trends** — seasonality and rising vs declining interest. A keyword peaking every December needs a different content calendar than a flat one.
- **Competitor gap** — put 2 to 3 ranking competitors into a gap tool and export keywords they rank for that the site does not.

Record volume, KD, and CPC in the template CSV.

## Phase 3: Classify intent

Every keyword gets exactly one label:

- **Informational** — the searcher wants to learn ("how to unclog a drain"). Target: guides and blog posts.
- **Commercial** — the searcher compares options ("best plumber austin", "tankless vs tank water heater"). Target: comparison and service pages.
- **Transactional** — the searcher wants to buy or book now ("emergency plumber near me", "book dental cleaning"). Target: location and booking pages.
- **Local** — the searcher wants a nearby provider. Often overlaps transactional; the Map Pack is the prize.

Intent decides the page type. Getting this wrong is the most common keyword research mistake: an informational keyword will never convert on a service page, and a transactional keyword will never rank with a blog post.

## Phase 4: Manual SERP analysis

For every shortlisted keyword, open the top 10 results and note:

1. **Who ranks** — big brands, directories (Yelp, Angi), forums (Reddit), or small independent sites? Small sites in the top 10 mean the keyword is winnable.
2. **Content type** — what format wins (listicle, service page, video, tool)? Match the winning format or beat it clearly.
3. **Content quality** — thin, outdated content in the top 5 is an opening.
4. **SERP features** — AI Overview, Map Pack, People Also Ask, videos. An AI Overview that fully answers the query pushes informational keywords down the priority list.
5. **Domain strength** — check DA/DR of ranking domains. Multiple weak domains (low authority, few referring domains) confirm a real opening.

## Phase 5: Prioritize

Score each keyword on four axes, then sort:

- **Volume** — enough searches to matter for the business.
- **Difficulty** — can this site realistically rank in 3 to 6 months?
- **Intent value** — transactional and local intent outrank informational for service businesses.
- **Business fit** — does the business actually want these customers?

Label P1 (target now), P2 (next quarter), P3 (later). A P1 keyword is high intent, winnable difficulty, and decent volume. Most lists end up with 10 to 20 P1 keywords, which is plenty for the first two quarters.

## Phase 6: Map to pages

- One primary keyword per page.
- Group keywords with identical intent and near-identical SERPs onto one page.
- Build the internal linking plan at the same time: pillar pages link down to clusters, clusters link up.

## Maintenance

Re-run phases 4 and 5 quarterly for P1 keywords. SERPs shift, competitors publish, Google ships new features. A keyword that was P1 in January can be P3 by July.
